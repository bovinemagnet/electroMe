= electroMe
:description: Work out which retail electricity plan actually costs you least, using your own half-hourly consumption.

electroMe answers one question: *which electricity plan actually costs you least, given how you
really use power?*

It reads the half-hourly interval data your retailer or distributor already holds about your
premises, prices it against as many tariffs as you care to define, and shows you where the money
goes.

== Why not just compare the advertised rates

Because a tariff is not a single number. A time-of-use plan charges differently depending on when
you use electricity, so two households on the same tariff pay very different effective rates. The
only way to know which plan suits *you* is to price *your* consumption, half hour by half hour.

The difference is not academic. On the data this software was built against, the 4pm–9pm window
accounts for *28% of the energy consumed and 42% of the bill*. A comparison that ignores timing
cannot see that at all.

== What it does

Compare plans::
Prices your real consumption against every tariff you define, ranks them cheapest first, and
breaks each bill into its components so you can see *where* the difference sits rather than only
which plan wins.

Explain the bill::
Charts your average load through the day with the tariff's price windows shaded behind it, so the
overlap between when you use power and when it is expensive is visible at a glance. Also shows
consumption by month and a full-resolution heatmap of every half hour.

Model what would change the answer::
Estimates what shifting load out of the peak window, adding solar, or adding a battery would be
worth per year — each re-costed against every plan, because part of a battery's value is that it
changes which tariff suits you.

== What it does not do

* *It is not a switching service.* It tells you what a tariff would have cost. Signing up is your
  business.
* *It does not fetch plans for you yet.* You define tariffs as small YAML files. Automatic
  harvesting of published market offers is planned but not built.
* *It is not financial advice.* The what-if figures are upper bounds from a simplified model —
  see xref:what-if.adoc#assumptions[the assumptions] before acting on them.
