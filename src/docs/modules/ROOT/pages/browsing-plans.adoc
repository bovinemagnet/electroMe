= Browsing plans
:description: Finding, filtering and inspecting any of the plans published for your network.

Harvesting the market gives you a couple of hundred tariffs. The dashboard answers the question
you arrived with; this screen is for the one you ask next.

Open it from the navigation, or from *Compare all plans* at the foot of the dashboard shortlist.

== Why the dashboard only shows a shortlist

Ranking two hundred plans in one table renders every row: metres of scrolling, and the single
most actionable fact — the cheapest plan you can actually sign up to — buried three rows in
amongst offers that need solar, a battery and an electric vehicle.

So the dashboard shows four kinds of plan and nothing else:

* your own tariff, and any other plan you have defined as a file
* the cheapest plan anyone can sign up to
* the cheapest plan that exists at all, with its requirement named beside it
* the regulated benchmark

Nothing is deleted. The rest moves here.

[#answer]
== The answer above the table

The table is evidence, not an answer. Above it sits one sentence: the cheapest plan you can
actually sign up to, what it would have cost you over the window you have selected, and what that
is worth against the plan you are actually on — the plan named by `electrome.baseline.plan`; see
xref:configuration.adoc#baseline[why that is configured rather than guessed]. Sometimes the
sentence is that you are already on it.

Two things about it are deliberate.

It never moves as you filter.::
The answer is drawn from *every plan costed*, never from the rows your search, retailer or shape
criteria have narrowed the table down to. Which plan is cheapest is a fact about the market, not
about what you happened to type into a search box, and an answer that shifted every time you
adjusted a filter would be worth nothing. Whenever a criterion is in force, the page says so
explicitly, rather than leaving you to notice the mismatch yourself.

Everything that qualifies the figure travels in the same sentence.::
A discount that has to be earned, equipment the plan requires, and — when a cheaper plan exists but
asks for something you have not said you own — an explicit admission that something cheaper was
passed over. None of that is a footnote below the fold. If it changes what the headline number is
worth, it belongs in the sentence stating the number, not underneath it.

Some of what a harvest returns cannot be priced at all — a plan the harvester refused rather than
guess at; see xref:market-plans.adoc[Market plans] for why. Those plans are not simply left out of
the ranking: the panel names how many there are and why, behind a disclosure you can open, because
one of them could be cheaper than the plan named above it. A gap you cannot see is not the same as
no gap at all.

== The criteria

All filtering happens on the server, over plans that have *already been costed*. Criteria change
what you are shown, never what was computed, so the counts on the page are always measured
against the full set.

Search::
Matches the plan name or the retailer, ignoring case.

Requirements::
Whether to include plans a household must own or join something to be offered.

Shape::
The structure of the tariff: flat, time-of-use, block or demand. A plan carrying several is
described by the most distinguishing one, so a time-of-use plan that also has a demand charge is
listed as a demand plan.

Sort by::
What it would cost, the peak rate, the daily supply charge, the effective rate, or the name.

Show::
How many rows to render. The default is bounded deliberately; *All* is a deliberate act.

Unconditional::
Hides every plan whose total depends on earning a discount — a loyalty price, a pay-on-time
saving, anything the retailer makes conditional. Tick it and the table shows only totals that hold
regardless of what you do next.

Saving at least::
Hides plans saving less than the amount you enter, measured against your own tariff. A plan with
nothing to measure against is kept rather than hidden: there is no saving figure to compare it on,
and dropping every such row for a reason you cannot see would be worse than showing them.

Every criterion appears in the URL, so a filtered view can be bookmarked or sent to somebody
else.

== Reading a row

A column of totals invites you to take the smallest number in it. Everything that would make that
a mistake travels with the row rather than living in a legend underneath the table.

Requirement chips::
Named after the plan, in the retailer's own wording — hover a chip to read the exact eligibility
text it was classified from.

An asterisk on the total::
The figure holds only if you earn a discount the plan makes conditional on something; hover it for
what earning it requires.

*Fees not costed*::
This plan publishes fees — a card surcharge, a paper-bill fee, a membership — that are not folded
into the total beside it; see <<fees,Fees and incentives>>.

*Partly priced*::
Some of your energy fell outside every charge this plan defines. Its total is a floor, not a
figure: the true cost is higher, by an amount nothing on the page can currently state.

[#requirements]
== Telling the page what you own

Of the plans a live harvest returns, roughly a fifth need equipment or a membership — solar, a
battery, an electric vehicle, a subscription — and the cheapest few nearly always do. Showing them
by default puts a number at the top of the page that you may have no way to reach. So the default
requirement filter is *Anyone can sign up*, the count of what that hides is always on screen, and
one click reveals them. This is an editorial position rather than a technical one, and it is the
only place this software takes one.

Under *I have*, tick solar, a battery, or an electric vehicle if the household owns them. This
does not add or remove a single row from the table — it changes which plan <<answer,the answer
above the table>> is allowed to name. Tick nothing, and the answer can only ever be a plan open to
anyone; tick solar, and it may name a plan that requires it, because now you could actually take
it up.

The published eligibility text has no structured way to say what it means — every entry in the
register is typed `OTHER` — so what a plan requires is classified from the wording itself: solar,
a battery, an electric vehicle, a membership, a concession card. Wording the classifier cannot
place becomes a requirement of its own, labelled *Conditions apply*, rather than being dropped.
That distinction matters: a requirement nobody could read is not the same as no requirement at
all, and treating it as none would let a plan into the answer on the strength of a condition
nobody actually checked.

NOTE: Ordinary conditions every plan carries — being in the right distribution zone, having a
smart meter, being a residential customer — are not treated as requirements. Flagging those would
mark almost every plan and therefore say nothing.

A plan you are not eligible for is never removed from the table, only from the answer above it. It
still appears as a row, with its chip, so you can see what you are missing and judge for yourself
whether it is worth pursuing — the table is the evidence, and evidence should not be hidden just
because it does not apply to you today.

== Opening a plan

Selecting a plan shows what a comparison row cannot: the tariff itself.

The 24-hour strip::
Every window drawn to scale across the day, coloured the same way as everywhere else on the site:
red is the dearest window, amber the middle of the day, blue a shoulder rate, green the cheapest.
A plan that prices weekdays and weekends differently gets a strip each — drawing both on one axis
would read as a tariff charging two rates at once. A window running past midnight is drawn at both
ends of the day, because that is where it falls.

Charges::
The daily supply charge, and every other component the plan carries.

This bill, line by line::
What the plan would have cost over the window you have selected, with the arithmetic behind it.

Eligibility::
Anything you must own or join, quoted in full rather than hidden behind a hover.

[#fees]
== Fees and incentives are shown but not costed

Retailers publish fees — card processing, paper bills, disconnection, membership — and incentives
such as sign-up credits and separate feed-in rates. Both are shown on the detail panel, and both
are explicitly labelled as *not included in the costed figures*.

They are not costed because published fee amounts are GST inclusive while unit prices are GST
exclusive. Folding them into the engine mixes two tax bases, and getting that subtly wrong is
worse than stating plainly that they are excluded.

Showing rates while silently omitting a ten-dollar monthly membership fee would mislead precisely
where you had gone looking for detail. So they are shown, and the exclusion is stated — and a plan
like this carries a *Fees not costed* marker beside its row in the table, before you ever open it.

== Comparing a few of them

Ticking two to four plans and pressing *Compare selected* opens
xref:comparing-plans.adoc[the comparison screen], which attributes the difference between them to
named charge components and says whether the ranking would survive a change in your consumption.

== Plans defined as files

A plan you have written yourself is marked *from a file*. The distinction matters: where a local
file and a harvested plan share an identifier, the local file wins, because it is usually the
tariff you are actually on and may no longer be published.

== The date window follows you

The window you select is carried on every link. Move between the dashboard and the browser and you
are still looking at the same period; reload, and it survives; copy the URL, and it describes
exactly what you were looking at.

== Why this stays fast at a few hundred plans

Every plan is costed independently of every other, and the costing engine keeps no state between
runs, so nothing stops them being costed at once rather than one after another. On this project's
own numbers — a household's real year of half-hourly data (35,036 intervals) against a full
AusNet harvest of 582 mapped plans — costing them in parallel took around 421 milliseconds,
against 680 milliseconds doing the same work one plan at a time.

Adjusting a criterion afterwards does not re-cost anything: filtering and sorting run over a
result that is already costed. Re-running the same costing a second time — reloading the page, say
— is served from a cache, at around 110 milliseconds: the time to build the page, not to price
582 tariffs again.

That cache is keyed on the usage series *by identity*, not by the numbers it holds, and that is
deliberate rather than an oversight. A what-if scenario that only rearranges when energy is used
can, by value, look identical to the series it was derived from; keying on identity means it is
never mistaken for that baseline and served the baseline's answer. The cache itself holds only a
handful of entries and is discarded wholesale once it fills, because a scenario series exists to
answer one question and is never asked again after that — see xref:what-if.adoc[What-if
modelling].

== Empty results

A set of criteria matching nothing never renders a blank table. The screen names every criterion
in force and offers to clear them.

If nothing has been defined *and* nothing harvested, it says so and points at the harvest control
instead — see xref:market-plans.adoc[Market plans].
